Wicket

Self-hosted mail, with a door on it

A wicket is the post-office service window — and the small gate beside a large one, that people pass through a single file. Both readings are the product: mail from a stranger waits at the window until you rule on it, and the window is on a server you own.

Direction
The Sorting Office. Not the philately of mail — stamps, envelopes, air-mail chevrons — but the back-of-house room where mail is actually processed: the sorting frame, the tray label, the facing slip, and the struck cancellation.
Thesis
The inbox is the room mail is processed in, not a feed of it. Triage state is struck across the item, never badged beside it. This refuses the arrangement every other client ships: rounded cards floating on grey with one indigo accent.
Register
Calm on first open, expert underneath. Nothing here reads as a homelab tool, and nothing is loud enough to tire you by four in the afternoon — except one room, on purpose.
Scope
Every screen the client renders, plus the repo-facing marks: favicon set, PWA icons, apple-touch-icon, preview card, README header. Built so a marketing surface can join later without a rebrand.

Two stocks the ground is the frame, not the paper

In a real sorting office the dominant surface is not kraft paper — kraft is the item. The room is painted steel and hardwood under fluorescent light. So the app's ground is the frame, and content sits in its compartments.

The strike the signature — state is struck across the item

Every other client tells you what happened to a thread by putting a chip beside it. A sorting office strikes the item itself.

A triage decision lands as an off-register overprint, blended into the row with mix-blend-mode so it reads as ink on stock rather than a sticker on glass. The tilt is derived from the thread id, so it never jitters between renders.

Wet, then set. An Optimistic Action strikes wet — faint, because the Sync Backend has not answered yet. When the server confirms, the ink sets. On rollback it lifts. The product's optimistic model, legible in the material.

Where it may appear, and where it may not. Only on state that persists: a search result you have already acted on, a held or blocked sender, a failed send, and the rollback toast where ink comes off. A triage keystroke strikes nothing — store/reads.ts drops an archived Thread from the list the moment the mutation is queued, so there is no row left to ink, and holding one open to show ink would tax the one thing that has to stay snap-instant. Read, star, pin and label are silent too: they happen constantly and reverse freely. Ink never accumulates down a list.

What the strike may never cost: a frame. It animates transform and opacity only, on the outgoing row, and never delays the next keystroke — press E four times fast and you get four instant advances with ink landing behind you. Under prefers-reduced-motion it appears at its final state with no rotation. In compact density it fades rather than tilts, because there is no room to tilt in.

The frame rules, not gaps · plates, not cards

Division
Compartments meet on 1px hairline rules that touch at the corners. Outer members are heavier than inner ones. There is no gutter between compartments and no shadow under them — a frame is joinery, not floating cards. This page is built that way; the app is the same frame.
Corners
2px on plates, 1px on bands, 0 on the frame. Metal label holders and wooden compartments have square corners. Nothing in this system is soft, and that one decision is most of what stops it reading as another SaaS inbox.
Heads
Every region is headed by an inset label-holder plate — recessed stock, condensed caps, a hairline under it. It is the whole heading; nothing sits above it.
Elevation
One shadow exists in the entire system, for things that genuinely float above the frame — popovers, dialogs, the label picker. It carries a real offset and a soft blur. Nothing else is lifted.
Rank
Importance is shown by inversion, not by brightness. The selected row and the primary action invert their stock; controls take no ink colour at all, only relief and rule. Borrowed as a discipline from the depot destination blind, where the lead panel alone prints dark on pale cloth.
Destruction
Trash and Block are one keystroke away in a triage app, so the design owes them distance: destructive controls sit in deliberate empty space and stay outline until focused.

Correspondent marks: a circular date stamp — a broken ring with initials, rotated deterministically from the address, so the same person carries the same mark forever. Drawn, never fetched: remote images are blocked until a sender is Approved, which makes a fetched avatar a contradiction. In the list this collapses to the small kraft plate on the stile; here it gets its full size.

Copy discipline: “Approve” and “Block” are the product’s own words for a Verdict, and the glossary outranks brand copy — “Admit” and “Return” read better and mean nothing in CONTEXT.md. The domain slip states the consequence before you act, because a Verdict on a whole domain routes future mail to Trash. Product vocabulary from CONTEXT.md is binding: Thread, Mail Account, Screener, Verdict — never conversation, mailbox, or quarantine.

Pictograms drawn solid on a 24 grid, not a line-icon set

Signage pictograms are solid and heavy so they survive being small, dirty and far away. The core triage actions get authored glyphs in that grammar — a tray, not an inbox arrow; a grommet, not a pushpin. Lucide stays installed for the long tail, and never appears beside one of these.

Repo-facing marks judged before the app is ever run

Someone decides whether to trust this with fifteen years of correspondence from a repository page, a GHCR listing, and a link preview in a chat app. So the postmark ships in four lockups, and the placeholder V icons and the unowned electric purple both go.

The mark: a circular date stamp broken at the lower left — the wicket you pass through single file — struck by killer bars, the cancellation that marks an item as accepted and processed. It is the mechanism, not a picture of an envelope, and at 16px it drops to two bars so the gap survives. The preview card is a tray label: the postmark on the night frame, the name at wdth 118, and one line of what it is.

Refusals what this system will not do, however convenient

Rounded cards on grey

The frame divides on hairline rules that meet at the corners. Radius stays at 2px, and no compartment floats.

A chip beside the row

State is struck across the item. A row restyles inside its own columns and never grows chrome next to it.

A coloured left border

No exception, not even for Labels. A bundle band's colour is the tint of the stock it is printed on, the whole way round — a stripe down one edge is the tell, not the material.

Fetched avatars

Remote images are blocked until a sender is Approved. Correspondent marks are drawn from the address.

A floating compose button

Compose is a plate on the frame, where the room's own controls live.

Motion on repeated actions

No entrance stagger on list rows, no animated reflow when a page grows. Those happen hundreds of times a session.

Monospace as costume

Martian Mono appears for codes, ids, addresses, timestamps and keycaps. Never for prose that wants to look technical.

A font CDN

Both faces are self-hosted from the app's origin. The product's promise is that nothing phones home.

Electric purple #7E14FF

The placeholder theme colour and the periwinkle --mail-accent both go. Aniline violet is chosen, not inherited.

Motion one authored moment, and a hard floor under it

The moment
The strike, and only the strike. 190ms on cubic-bezier(.16,.9,.12,1), transform and opacity only, on the row that is leaving.
Never animated
J/K navigation, list reflow when a page grows, density changes, and anything that happens more than a few times a minute. Speed here is the product, and the existing row-entrance stagger is removed for this reason.
Press
Controls take a 120ms scale to 0.96 — kept from the current build, because a physical control that gives nothing back feels broken.
Reduced motion
The strike appears at its final state with no rotation and no scale. Nothing is hidden by animation timing, so nothing can go missing.
